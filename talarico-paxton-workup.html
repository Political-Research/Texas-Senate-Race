Where the race stands

James Talarico (D), a four-term state representative from Austin, won the March 3 Democratic primary over U.S. Rep. Jasmine Crockett, 52.8% to 45.9%. Ken Paxton (R), the three-term attorney general, finished narrowly behind Sen. John Cornyn in the March primary, then won the May 26 runoff with 63.8% after Trump endorsed him the week before, calling him a "true MAGA warrior" PBS/AP. Libertarian Ted Brown is also on the ballot.

Context that cuts both ways: Republicans have not lost a statewide race in Texas in 32 years ProPublica/Texas Tribune, yet the Cook Political Report rates this race a toss-up The Hill.

Money and outside spending

Through June 30, Talarico's committee had raised about $68.6 million and Paxton's two committees about $9.2 million, roughly 7 to 1 per FEC filings as reported. Cash on hand at that point is reported differently: $21.5 million in that account, $23.3 million in the Texas Tribune tracker, against about $1.7 million for Paxton The Texan. I did not open the FEC filings to settle it.

The gap narrows once outside money is counted. Paxton's campaign has received close to $54 million from the Senate Leadership Fund's Texas affiliate, and Trump's super PAC added $5 million The Hill. A Texas Tribune headline from Sept. 22 reports Senate Republicans raising their ad buy for Paxton to $100 million (headline only; I did not read the story). On the Democratic side, a Talarico-aligned super PAC, Lone Star Rising, had raised around $9 million by spring Statesman, and Schumer's Senate Majority PAC has launched a Texas-focused PAC Washington Examiner.

What voters say matters

In the UT/Texas Politics Project poll, Texas registered voters named inflation (19%) and political corruption or leadership (15%) as the state's top problems, ahead of the economy and immigration (10% each) topline On the record. That combination explains both campaigns' emphasis: cost of living from both sides, corruption from Talarico, and immigration and social issues from Paxton.

The candidates

James Talarico (D)

Former middle school teacher and seminarian, 36 at the time of the primary, who built a national profile pairing progressive policy with openly Christian language TIME. First elected to the Texas House in 2018 by flipping a Republican seat Nexstar; named one of Texas Monthly's ten best legislators NBC 5. Reported net worth $67,000–$305,000 ProPublica.

Ken Paxton (R)

Texas attorney general since 2015 after serving in the Legislature from 2003 ProPublica. Built his profile as the leading state-level legal opponent of the Biden administration on immigration, abortion and gender policy PBS/AP. Impeached by the Texas House in 2023 and acquitted by the Senate; never convicted of anything. Reported net worth $1 million–$27 million ProPublica.

The case for and against each

These are the strongest arguments each side's supporters and critics make, with the evidence behind them where it exists. Listing an argument is not endorsing it.

Talarico

Case for
  • Money and organization. His campaign reports over $70 million from 780,000 individual donors, a record second quarter Texas Tribune (campaign-reported figures).
  • Trust edge. 46% say "honest and trustworthy" describes him well, versus 33% for Paxton; his net favorability is +10 to Paxton's −13 UT/TPP.
  • Clean finances. Modest, stable net worth and no legal cases against him.
  • Crossover pitch. Calls himself a "border security Democrat," backs oil and gas jobs, and has drawn some Republican support Dallas Morning News.
  • Youth vote. Leads by 15 among voters under 50 in Emerson's August poll Emerson.
Case against
  • Repositioning. Positions and tone on ICE, capitalism, fossil fuels and gender have shifted sharply since 2020 and since the primary; his campaign concedes some past remarks missed the mark. Critics call it a makeover (see Tensions).
  • Money-in-politics message vs. practice. Runs against billionaire and dark money while a billionaire- and dark-money-funded super PAC works for him.
  • His own voting-address problem undercuts his attack on Paxton over the same issue.
  • Unpriced agenda. Minimum wage to $15, public option, expanded credits, with no full cost estimate (Paxton's plan has none either) FOX 26.
  • Structural headwinds. No Democrat has won statewide in 32 years; a large gender gap (56% of women vs. 36% of men in a June NYT/Siena poll) The 19th/Texas Tribune.

Paxton

Case for
  • Base and Trump. Trump's endorsement and a 28-point runoff win over an incumbent senator Brookings.
  • A decade of conservative litigation. Suits against the Biden administration on immigration, abortion and gender policy; supporters see a fighter who delivers PBS/AP.
  • Never convicted. Acquitted by the Texas Senate; federal prosecutors dropped their case; state securities charges dismissed after a pretrial agreement. Supporters frame the cases as political persecution.
  • Concrete tax plan. Specific deduction amounts for medical costs and home buying, plus doubling the child tax credit FOX 26.
  • Texas fundamentals. Republicans hold 50% party ID to Democrats' 40% among registered voters, and Trump carried the sample's 2024 voters 47–36 UT/TPP.
Case against
  • Trust deficit. 41% of registered voters say "honest and trustworthy" describes him "not at all" UT/TPP.
  • Accumulated ethics record. Impeachment, a nine-year securities case, a $6.6 million whistleblower judgment paid by taxpayers, questioned financial disclosures, and a questioned voting address (see Tensions).
  • Party unity. 84% of Republicans back him vs. 99% of Democrats for Talarico in the TSU poll FOX 7; Cornyn voters were the question from the start Texas Tribune.
  • Health care exposure. Led the 2018 multistate suit to strike down the entire ACA, dismissed 7–2 for lack of standing Texas Standard, in a state where 64% are very concerned about health costs UT/TPP.
  • Dependence on outside money. Joint fundraising committees supplied about 45% of his haul vs. 8% of Talarico's Texas Tribune.

How they match up on the big issues

Positions as stated by the candidates or documented in their records. Where I could only find a position through a partisan outlet, that's noted.

Cost of living and taxes

TalaricoA $15 federal minimum wage, a bigger child tax credit and EITC, and repeal of tariffs he says raise prices FOX 26. Pays for it partly by repealing Republican tax cuts for the wealthy Houston Public Media. Also proposes medical debt cancellation and homebuying help Dallas Morning News.

Paxton"Protecting the Texas Promise": a $50,000 deduction for first-time homebuyers, up to $25,000 per person for out-of-pocket medical costs, a $5,000 deduction for health-related spending, doubling the child tax credit, and making "Trump Accounts" permanent FOX 26.

Overlap: both want a larger child tax credit. Contrast: Paxton works through deductions, which help most those with taxable income to deduct against; Talarico works through wages, credits and programs. Neither has published a full cost estimate.

Health care

TalaricoExtend ACA premium subsidies, create a public option, lower drug prices and regulate pharmacy benefit managers FOX 26.

PaxtonThe medical-expense deductions above. As attorney general he led the 20-state suit to strike down the whole ACA, which the Supreme Court dismissed 7–2 on standing in 2021 Texas Standard On the record.

The sharpest structural contrast in the race: expand the ACA framework versus shift support into the tax code.

Immigration and the border

TalaricoNow calls himself a "border security Democrat" and says he criticized Biden on the border Fox News. Platform: focus ICE on public safety and national security, ban masked agents, more immigration judges, modernized ports of entry, and legal status for some long-term undocumented residents Fox News. In January he called for taking ICE money back to communities Fortune.

PaxtonClose alignment with Trump on enforcement; as attorney general he sued the Biden administration repeatedly over immigration policy PBS/AP. Paxton says Talarico voted against every border security bill Fox News; I did not check that against the House journal.

Texas voters are split: ICE is viewed 41% favorable, 46% unfavorable, while 51% agree undocumented immigrants should be deported immediately UT/TPP. Both candidates have room to argue they're with the majority.

Abortion

TalaricoSupports codifying Roe-era protections in federal law and has consistently voted against abortion restrictions in the Legislature; frames his support in terms of his faith The 19th/Texas Tribune.

PaxtonAmong the most anti-abortion officials in the country: sued to block federal emergency-abortion guidance in Texas, threatened hospitals in the Kate Cox case, sued out-of-state providers mailing pills, and urged Congress to override blue-state shield laws. Has not said what he'd pursue in the Senate; Texas Right to Life suggests a 15- or 20-week national limit The 19th/Texas Tribune.

Neither campaign is leading with it. 47% of Texas registered voters call themselves pro-choice and 40% pro-life UT/TPP.

Guns

TalaricoCampaign site says he'll protect the Second Amendment while reducing gun violence. Voted against permitless carry in 2021; rated F by the NRA Daily Caller News Foundation.

PaxtonRated A by the NRA (same source).

Single-sourced through a partisan outlet citing Vote Smart and an NRA spokesperson. The direction is not in doubt; the specific grades are unverified by me.

Energy

TalaricoSays he'll protect oil and gas jobs and backs an all-of-the-above strategy Red River Radio. In 2020 he predicted the fossil fuel industry wouldn't last (see Tensions).

PaxtonLongtime defender of the oil and gas industry who used his office to fight federal rules E&E News/Politico.

Elections and voting

TalaricoCites Texas's Republican secretary of state that elections are safe and secure; calls Paxton's fraud prosecutions a witch hunt ProPublica/Texas Tribune.

PaxtonHas made voter-fraud enforcement a signature: helped oust high-court judges who blocked him from prosecuting election crimes unilaterally and set up a fraud tip line before the primary (same source).

Both now face questions about their own voting addresses. See Tensions.

Ethics and money in politics

TalaricoWould ban super PACs and stock trading by members of Congress; refuses corporate PAC money Texas Tribune.

PaxtonCasts the cases against him as political attacks. I found no reform platform on ethics from his campaign.

Relationship to Trump

TalaricoWants tariffs repealed FOX 26; backed impeachment proceedings against former DHS Secretary Kristi Noem in the primary Fortune.

PaxtonTrump-endorsed and closely aligned on immigration, elections and executive power; Trump has said he'll return to campaign for him Gray DC.

Where each is in tension with his own stated values

Each item below measures a candidate against a value he himself has claimed, not against his opponents' values. That rule kept some popular attacks out: for example, the argument that Talarico's support for abortion contradicts his Christianity, or that Paxton's politics contradict someone else's reading of the Gospels. Those are disagreements about theology, not inconsistencies.

The items differ in kind. Paxton's rest mostly on court records and investigative reporting; several of Talarico's are shifts in position that he partly acknowledges. Two more Paxton items are real but couldn't be tied to a value he has stated himself, so they sit in a separate note below rather than being forced into the format. Don't read the count as a score.

Paxton

Disputes the facts in public, declined to contest them in court

On the record
Stated position
Called the whistleblower judgment ridiculous and not based on the facts or the law, and vowed to appeal CBS Texas.
Record
In court, his office stopped contesting the facts, allegations similar to those his lawyers had fought hard during the impeachment trial. The judge found retaliation against four aides who reported him to the FBI and awarded $6.6 million, which taxpayers pay Texas Tribune. He appealed, then dropped the appeal; plaintiffs' lawyers said conceding let him avoid answering questions under oath Texas Tribune.
His response
Calls it a judgment for baseless claims by rogue employees and blames the House leadership that impeached him.
How strong
Strong on what happened in court. It does not prove the underlying bribery allegations: he was acquitted of all 16 impeachment articles and federal prosecutors dropped their case. The motive for conceding (avoiding testimony) is the plaintiffs' characterization.

Election-fraud enforcer, questioned voting address

Reported
Stated value
Strict enforcement of election law, including against voters who misstate their residence; his office publicly warned before the primary that misrepresenting your residence on election records is illegal ProPublica/Texas Tribune.
Record
ProPublica and the Texas Tribune found he voted six times over two years while registered at a Collin County home his estranged wife says he hadn't lived in for two years; reporting links him to a Denton County home since February ProPublica/Texas Tribune.
His response
His campaign says he's a lawful registered voter and has repeatedly declined detailed questions.
How strong
Records-based reporting, not adjudicated. An ethics lawyer quoted doubts either candidate committed prosecutable fraud (intent is required) but argues the state's chief election enforcer should be held to a higher standard. Talarico faces a similar finding.

Federalism, except on abortion shield laws

Interpretive
Stated value
Chaired the Republican Attorneys General Association, whose stated mission includes defending federalism, and argued his ACA suit would return health decisions to the states Houston Chronicle, 2019.
Record
Signed a letter urging Congress to pass legislation overriding other states' shield laws for abortion providers The 19th/Texas Tribune.
His response
None found. Supporters would say protecting unborn life outranks federalism, which is a coherent position.
How strong
Facts are solid; whether it's inconsistency depends on how you rank those values.

Three ethics experts said unreported income would violate federal disclosure law; his campaign calls it manufactured controversy; the Senate Ethics Committee has made no finding ProPublica/Texas Tribune.

Adultery allegations On the record Disputed An extramarital affair was aired in testimony at his 2023 impeachment trial. In July 2025 his wife, state Sen. Critics frame this as at odds with his faith-based politics, but I didn't find a specific statement of his on marriage to measure it against. I also didn't confirm whether the divorce is final.

Talarico

Against big money, backed by big money

On the record Interpretive
Stated value
Campaigns against billionaire influence and dark money and would ban super PACs Texas Tribune; pledged a positive primary campaign.
Record
Lone Star Rising, a super PAC backing him, raised around $9 million from wealthy donors, nearly half via a group that doesn't disclose donors, with Reid Hoffman its largest individual donor Statesman. It ran negative ads against Crockett while his own ads stayed positive Texas Tribune. He had also accepted $54,500 from an Adelson-linked PAC in an earlier cycle (single-sourced, via a fact-check aggregator citing the Austin Independent).
His response
He can't legally control super PACs, won't "unilaterally disarm," and would ban them if elected; his campaign says it had no involvement in the ads Texas Tribune.
How strong
The money is documented. Whether it's hypocrisy depends on whether you accept the "don't disarm alone" argument, which reformers in both parties make. He has not publicly asked the PAC to stop.

Accused Paxton of voter fraud, same issue in his own record

Reported
Stated value
Said Paxton "was committing voter fraud the whole time" over his voting address ProPublica/Texas Tribune.
Record
Bought a home in June 2022 but kept his parents' address, about six miles away, on his registration until September 2024, voting in five elections in that span (same source). The same reporting found records supporting his residency for his 2022 House eligibility, contradicting an earlier conservative-outlet claim.
His response
Declined to say when he moved in, citing safety threats, and called the questions a Paxton deflection.
How strong
Same evidentiary footing as the Paxton item. A political scientist quoted says it supports a hypocrisy charge against either man.

What I did and didn't verify

  • The UT/Texas Politics Project August 2026 topline (poll numbers, trust ratings, issue priorities, party ID).
  • Emerson's August release.
  • Two ProPublica/Texas Tribune investigations in full: the voting-address story and the financial-disclosure story. These are the root source for most of the address and disclosure material; other outlets repeating them are not independent confirmation.
  • The FOX 26 side-by-side of both economic plans, built from the campaigns' own releases, and The 19th/Texas Tribune abortion piece in full.

Secondary only

  • Most polls, all fundraising figures (I did not open FEC filings), primary and runoff results, and the whistleblower and securities case histories come from news reports, not the underlying filings.
  • NRA grades and Talarico's 2021 gun votes: single-sourced through a partisan outlet.
  • Talarico's 2020 Sierra Club remarks and the "missed the mark" response: seen only through Fox News and the Washington Examiner citing CNN.
  • The Adelson-linked PAC contribution: single-sourced through a fact-check aggregator.

Not researched, deliberately left out, or open

  • Attack lines I saw but didn't investigate: a plea deal Paxton's office reached in a child sex-abuse case, a Texas Tech donor story, and Republican ads on Talarico's past remarks about gender and religion. None is scored here.
